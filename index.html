Sourcing Strategy Generator

Describe the sourcing requirement, commercial context, supplier position and key risks. The tool will produce a structured procurement strategy aligned with the relevant category portfolio.

1. Requirement

Enter the relevant CPV code (you can search here https://cpvcodes.eu/en). The code determines which category portfolio guidance will be applied to the recommendation.

Assess the requirement’s relative commercial importance to your organization. Do not use expected spend alone; consider business impact, urgency, dependency, and consequences of failure.

Select whether this is a routine repeat purchase (straight rebuy), a repeat purchase with changed requirements (modified rebuy), or a genuinely new requirement (new task). More here https://marketing-dictionary.org/b/buy-classes/

Indicate how clearly the requirement is defined: not developed, partially developed, well developed, or fully approved. Less mature specifications normally require additional discovery before the sourcing process begins.

2. Stakeholders and objectives

Assess the degree of agreement between Procurement and the internal requirement owners. Select the option that best reflects whether priorities, scope, timing, and decision criteria are aligned.

You may select multiple options.

You may select multiple options.

3. Supplier and market

Assess how many realistic and qualified suppliers could compete for this requirement: Low means few viable alternatives, Moderate means some competition, and High means a strong supplier market.

Classify the category by commercial impact and supply risk: Routine, Leverage, Bottleneck, or Strategic. More here https://www.cips.org/intelligence-hub/supplier-relationship-management/kraljic-matrix

Assess how the incumbent supplier is likely to view your organization as a customer, based on your account value and attractiveness. Core means a strategically important customer; Development means a customer worth growing; Exploitable means commercially valuable but not strategically important; Nuisance means low value and low attractiveness. Select “N/A - No incumbent supplier” when there is no incumbent supplier.

4. Constraints and risks

Describe any practical constraints or concerns that could affect the sourcing strategy, such as tight deadlines, operational dependency, budget limits, transition risks, internal capability gaps, or excessive reliance on the incumbent supplier.
